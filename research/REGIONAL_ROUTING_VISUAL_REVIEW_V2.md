# Regional routing v2 — diagnostic visual review

Reviewed locally on 20 September 2026: all eight central-face mixed-damage contact sheets (four development identities, both fixed seeds), plus identity 1306's eye-removal engineering sheet at seed 17. Each sheet displays the clean evaluation target, damaged input and all six policies. These photographs stay local. This is an unblinded assistant inspection, not an independent human preference study or a quantitative success rate.

The mixed-damage sheets show conspicuous expression and feature changes across all policies:

- Group 1306: changed gaze, eye shape, mouth opening and lip appearance; seed 29 produces markedly different lips and nose appearance.
- Group 2790: eye asymmetry and altered mouth shape remain; seed 29 shows unnatural eye coloration/detail.
- Group 1043: smile, teeth and eye contours depart from the target; skin texture is smoothed.
- Group 787: altered eye size/gaze and mouth opening; bright nose detail and expression shifts remain at seed 29.

The routed policies look very similar to one another on these sheets. Differences between seeds are often more conspicuous than differences between routing policies. The eye-removal sheet likewise shows changed gaze and eye contours. These observations do not establish a systematic preference between methods.

There is no visual basis in this review to label regional routing a solved facial-fidelity improvement. Keep it in the research comparison rather than promote it as a better website mode. Numerical evaluation must retain all policies and seeds; any favorable average must be discussed alongside these failures.

The next bounded quality study should distinguish complete removal from degradation with surviving information. The present common strength of 0.99 may discard useful low-frequency evidence in blurred/noisy regions; that is a hypothesis to test under a new development protocol, not an explanation proven by this run. Denoising-strength and conditioning-scale calibration, visible-region consistency and reference alignment require matched controls before broader claims. Genuine spatial feature transfer and external reference-inpainting comparisons remain outstanding.
